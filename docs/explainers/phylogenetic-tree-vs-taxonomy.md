# Phylogentic trees vs Taxonomic trees

See [Phylogentic tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylogenetic_tree) on Wikipedia and [Taxonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxonomy) for more indepth explanations.

A taxonomic tree shows how humans classify organisms into ranks such as family, genus, and species. Historically, these ranks were based on morphology — how animals look and behave — because DNA was not available. As a result, taxonomy sometimes groups animals in ways that do not perfectly reflect their evolutionary history.

A phylogenetic tree, on the other hand, is built from DNA (and sometimes morphology). It shows how species actually evolved from common ancestors. Depending on how the tree is drawn:
- A phylogram shows the amount of molecular change (branch lengths = genetic difference).
- A chronogram shows time (branch lengths = millions of years).
- A cladogram shows only branching order (branch lengths have no meaning).

For example (see image below), in taxonomy, lions (Panthera leo) and tigers (Panthera tigris) are placed in the same genus, which correctly indicates they are closely related. Clouded leopards (Neofelis) are placed in the same subfamily (Pantherinae), meaning they share a more distant ancestor with Panthera species.
One of the morphological traits that correlates to difference the Panthera and the Felinea is the flexiblity of a specific neckbone (Hyoid) which determines if a cat can roar (Panthera) or meow (Felinea), but this trait does not define evolutionary closeness and it is not the only reason for the split in two families.

![](https://bigcatswildcats.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/big-cats-bio-classifcation-chart-768x543.png)

However, taxonomy cannot tell us how different lions and clouded leopards are genetically, or when their lineages split. A phylogenetic tree can.

Modern DNA studies show that:

- Clouded leopards (Neofelis nebulosa and Neofelis diardi) are distinct species with both nuclear and mitochondrial differences.
- Cheetahs (Acinonyx), pumas (Puma), and domestic cats (Felis) belong to the Felinae lineage and are more closely related to each other than any of them are to leopards or lions.

Phylogenetic trees allow us to see not only who is related, but how much they differ and when their lineages diverged — information that taxonomy alone cannot provide.

**Example of how taxonomy can change:**

All seaslugs where considered to be in the order Nudibranchia (naked gills) even when they lacked gills. Study was needed and recently (2025) a whole group seaslugs was moved from suborder Doridina up to a new order Doridida. It didn't change phylograms for these beautifull slugs, but the taxonomy changed.
